Sixty months, and what each of them asks of you.

Three years of junior residency with a sponsored Master of Public Health inside them. Two years of senior residency, an examination, and one piece of work with your name first on it.

At a glance

The six facts that decide whether the rest of this page is worth your time.

Duration
Sixty months, full time. Five years from entry to the exit assessment.
Junior residency
Years 1 to 3.
Senior residency
Years 4 to 5.
Master of Public Health
Read at the NUS Saw Swee Hock School of Public Health, fully sponsored, and completed during junior residency. Occupational Health is an available specialisation within the degree.
Exit pathways
Two. Public Health, or Occupational Medicine.

Ten, across eight participating organisations: Ministry of Health; Health Promotion Board; Ministry of Manpower; Ministry of Home Affairs; Singapore Armed Forces; National Healthcare Group; SingHealth; and the National University Health System.

The commitments people ask about

The eight questions the Programme Office is asked most often, answered where the answer is confirmed and marked where it is not.

On-call and weekends
Programme Office to supply: whether residents are rostered for call during hospital rotations, how often, and what weekend cover is required in each of the five years.
Where you work, by year
Postings sit in three settings: a ministry office, a statutory board or agency, and a hospital. Programme Office to supply: how the sixty months divide between ministry, agency and hospital settings, year by year.
Travel
Programme Office to supply: expected travel between sites during a normal week, and whether any posting requires relocation or extended time away from Singapore.
Locum and moonlighting
Programme Office to supply: whether locum work is permitted, the approval route, any cap on hours, and whether the answer differs between junior and senior residency.
Deferment
Programme Office to supply: the grounds on which entry or a training year may be deferred, who decides, and the effect on the service obligation.
Parental leave
Programme Office to supply: the entitlement, how training time is credited on return, and the effect on the sixty-month duration.
Overseas attachment
Programme Office to supply: whether an overseas attachment is available, its length, and who funds it.
Protected academic time
The Master of Public Health is read during junior residency and is fully sponsored. Programme Office to supply: the protected hours each week for coursework in Years 1 to 3, and what academic time exists in Years 4 and 5.

Nothing in this list is inferred. An answer appears here only once the Programme Office has confirmed it in writing. A marked gap means the answer has not been confirmed, not that the answer is unfavourable.

The five years

Three gates in junior residency, four requirements at exit. Nothing here is passed by attendance.

Junior residency, Years 1 to 3

Length
Thirty-six months.
What happens
Core rotations across the participating organisations, alongside the Master of Public Health at the NUS Saw Swee Hock School of Public Health. The degree is fully sponsored and is completed inside these three years. Occupational Health is an available specialisation within it.
Postings
Programme Office to supply: the junior residency rotation sequence, and the minimum months required at each site.
What you must pass
Three gates, all required to progress to senior residency. Pass the Master of Public Health. Pass the Progression Preventive Medicine Competency Committee Review. Receive the Programme Director's certification of successful completion of R3.

Senior residency, Years 4 to 5

Length
Twenty-four months.
What happens
Advanced rotations, taken at two or more sites in different participating organisations. You cannot spend senior residency inside a single organisation.
Pathway
You exit through one of two pathways: Public Health, or Occupational Medicine.
What you must pass
Four requirements, all required to exit. Advanced rotations at two or more sites in different organisations. A written examination. An oral assessment, taken as a viva voce. A first-author publication, or a technical or process report.
Accreditation
Accreditation wording to be supplied and signed off by the Sponsoring Institution: the accrediting body, the status held, and the date it was last reviewed. Nothing is stated here until it is.

Progression gates and exit requirements as set out in the programme brochure. The two exit pathways are as published by NUHS.

Research and scholarly activity

One of the four exit requirements is a piece of work with your name first on it. It is not optional and it is not a poster.

You cannot exit this residency without producing something. The requirement is a first-author publication, or a technical or process report — the second option exists because a great deal of consequential public health work is never written up for a journal, and the programme counts it anyway.

Singapore's public health instruments are the genre this requirement points at: Nutri-Grade beverage labelling, introduced in 2022; the national childhood immunisation schedule; permissible noise exposure limits; hawker centre hygiene grading. None of those is resident work. They are what the output looks like at national scale, and they are the reason a technical report counts equally with a paper.

The requirement
One first-author output: a publication, or a technical or process report.
Assessed with
It sits alongside the written examination and the oral assessment as one of the exit requirements of senior residency.
When it must be done
Programme Office to supply: the point in the five years by which the output must be submitted or accepted, and whether acceptance or publication is required.
Supervision
Programme Office to supply: who supervises the work, whether protected time is allocated to it, and what support exists for residents publishing for the first time.

Exit requirements as set out in the programme brochure.
