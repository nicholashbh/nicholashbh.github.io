Does the sponsored MPH bond me?

Yes. The sponsorship carries an additional service obligation of two years, served after specialist accreditation, and it runs consecutively to any existing MOH or PSC bond rather than concurrently — so your total obligation is the sum of the two, not the longer of them. The sponsored amount is S$48,000, the full cost of the part-time Master of Public Health at NUS Saw Swee Hock School of Public Health, including examination fees. If you leave early, liquidated damages are pro-rated against the unserved portion; fees already paid are not recovered.

Ask for the bond instrument itself and read it before you accept a place. This paragraph is a summary, and a summary is not the contract.

Can I return to clinical practice if I leave?

We are not publishing an answer to this until it is exact. It is the question prospective applicants ask us more than any other, and what turns on it is your registration status and your options at each exit point — leaving in Year 2 is not the same as leaving in Year 4. A paraphrase here could cost someone a career.

Needed: the position of the Singapore Medical Council and the Specialists Accreditation Board on entering another residency after exiting Preventive Medicine residency, stated for each exit point and confirmed in writing.

Until that is written here, put the question to the Programme Director directly. You will get the same answer we intend to publish.

How does this compare with Internal Medicine or Family Medicine?

The unit of care is a population rather than a person. Internal Medicine and Family Medicine treat the patient in front of you, and the result of the decision arrives inside the consultation or the admission. Preventive Medicine works on the conditions that produce those consultations — Nutri-Grade labelling on beverages, the national childhood immunisation schedule, permissible noise exposure limits at a worksite, hawker centre hygiene grading — and the result arrives years later, usually without a face attached to it.

Both are full-time specialist training. The trade is reach in exchange for immediacy and clinical continuity, and it is worth being honest with yourself about which of those you actually want.

Do my prior Medical Officer years let me skip R1?

Plan on the full sixty months. The programme is structured as sixty months of full-time training — Junior Residency in Years 1 to 3, Senior Residency in Years 4 and 5 — and the junior years carry the Master of Public Health and the progression review, neither of which prior Medical Officer service substitutes for.

Time spent as a Medical Officer is not wasted: it is what makes you credible at interview and useful in the work. It is simply not published as advanced standing. Needed: whether any mechanism for credit or advanced standing exists, who decides it, and on what evidence.

Am I eligible as a conditionally registered international medical graduate?

We will not guess at this one. Eligibility for residency in Singapore is set by the registration category you hold with the Singapore Medical Council and by MOH Holdings, not by this programme, and the answer is different for conditional, provisional and full registration.

Needed: the registration categories accepted for entry, any additional requirement placed on conditionally registered applicants, and the MOH Holdings reference that states it.

If you hold conditional registration and are considering applying, write to the Programme Director well before the interview registration window rather than after it. This is a question with a definite answer, and it should not be the thing that costs you a cycle.

Do I need an MPH before applying?

No. The Master of Public Health is part of the programme, not a prerequisite for it. It is fully sponsored and read during junior residency at NUS Saw Swee Hock School of Public Health, with Occupational Health available as a specialisation within the degree. Applying with no formal public health qualification is the ordinary case, and it is what most residents do.

Holding a public health master's degree already does not disqualify you, and on the published structure it does not shorten the programme. Needed: how an existing public health master's degree is treated — exemption, substitution, or neither.

Is the MPH full-time or part-time?

Part-time, alongside your postings. It is read during junior residency rather than in a year out of training, so you are carrying coursework and a rotation in the same week. Passing it is a gate rather than an ornament: junior residency does not end without it.

If you are heading for the Occupational Medicine exit pathway, Occupational Health is the specialisation to take within the degree. Needed: which of Years 1 to 3 the coursework falls in, and the expected weekly commitment during term.

Do I still do clinical work, and how much?

Yes, but it is not the bulk of your week. Clinical competence is one of the programme's selection criteria and stays under assessment throughout, so clinical practice is retained rather than dropped. The realistic picture is that most of your five years is spent outside a ward — on policy, surveillance, evaluation, regulation and programme work — and that is the point of the specialty rather than a defect in it.

Needed: the actual clinical commitment in months per year for each of Years 1 to 5, and which postings carry it.

What are the on-call and weekend commitments?

It varies by posting, and we will not publish a single number we cannot stand behind. A ministry or statutory board rotation does not carry the roster of a healthcare cluster or a Singapore Armed Forces posting, and an outbreak response can rearrange a week without notice.

Needed: typical call frequency and weekend commitment for each rotation type, and what is expected of residents during a public health emergency activation.

If this is your deciding factor — and for applicants with young children it often is — ask a current resident what the roster looked like last month, not what it looks like on paper.

Can I locum or moonlight?

That is governed by your employer's policy, not by the programme, and we are not going to summarise a policy we do not own. Residents are employed through the Sponsoring Institution and are bound by its rules on outside clinical work; separate approval is commonly required for anything paid.

Needed: the Sponsoring Institution's policy on locum and outside clinical work for residents — whether it is permitted, what approval is required, and any cap on hours.

Ask before you take a shift rather than after. The answer is administrative rather than difficult, but it is not ours to give.

What if I miss the interview registration window?

You wait for the next intake. Interview registration with the Sponsoring Institution is a separate and earlier step than the MOH Holdings eResidency application, and it is the deadline applicants miss — an open portal is not evidence that you are still in the cycle. For the July 2027 intake, interview registration closes 30 September 2026 and the eResidency portal closes 20 November 2026. There is no late route between the two.

If you are reading this after the registration date has passed, write to the Programme Office anyway and ask to be told when the next cycle opens.

How many places are there each intake?

Four or five. Across the last eight intakes the programme admitted 35 residents in total, the smallest cohort three and the largest five. The number is not fixed year to year. Whatever else this means for you, plan on a cohort small enough that you will know every member of it by name.

What happens if I do not pass a progression review?

You do not enter senior residency until the gate is cleared. Junior residency ends at three of them: passing the Master of Public Health, passing the Progression Preventive Medicine Competency Committee Review, and the Programme Director's certification that Year 3 was completed successfully. All three are required, and clearing two of the three is not progression.

What follows a failure — remediation, a repeat attempt, an extension of training, or exit — is handled case by case, and we are not going to generalise it here. Needed: attempts permitted at each gate, the remediation route, and the point at which exit is the outcome. If training is extended, ask what that does to the bond timeline before assuming it is unchanged.

Can I switch between the Public Health and Occupational Medicine pathways?

We do not publish the switching rule, so do not assume either that a change is free or that it is impossible. Both exit pathways sit inside the same sixty-month programme; the divergence is in which senior residency rotations you take and which exit assessment you sit. Occupational Medicine is the natural continuation if you specialise in Occupational Health within the Master of Public Health and rotate through Ministry of Manpower and Singapore Armed Forces postings.

Needed: when the pathway is declared, whether it can be changed after that point, who approves the change, and what it costs in training time.

What does the exit assessment consist of?

Four requirements, all of which must be met to complete senior residency: advanced rotations at two or more sites in different organisations; a written examination; an oral assessment, the viva voce; and a first-author publication or a technical or process report.

The written work is the requirement applicants underestimate. It has to be a piece of work of your own, begun early enough in senior residency to be finished on time, not a late write-up of something you helped with. The rotation requirement is the reason the ten sites across eight organisations matter operationally rather than decoratively.

Exit requirements as published by NUHS and set out in the programme brochure. Confirm the current wording of each requirement, and whether the publication may be a report in every case.

What do graduates actually do afterwards?

Public health and occupational health posts in government and in the healthcare clusters — but the specifics belong on the alumni page rather than in a generalisation here. Of 38 graduates since 2011, 36 have been traced to a current post. Four graduates are named publicly in the programme brochure: Dr Nicholas Hang (2025), Dr Sean Lim (2024), Dr Yin Zhien (2022) and Dr Leonara Liu (2018).

Needed: the current post of each named graduate, published with their consent, and the breakdown of the 36 traced posts by organisation.
