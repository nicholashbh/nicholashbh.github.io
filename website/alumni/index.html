Four graduates, the same six fields

Named in the programme brochure. Each profile answers the same six questions in the same order, whether or not the answer flatters the programme.

A profile page that lets each subject choose what to disclose produces four glowing paragraphs and settles nothing. The fixed schema below is the defence against that. Identical fields, identical order, and a visible gap wherever an answer has not been supplied — so if a field is full on one profile and empty on another, you can see it, and you can ask why.

Cohort years are taken from the programme brochure and are real. Every other field is a placeholder. Posts, projects and quotations will be published only once the named alumnus has approved the exact wording in writing, because a job title or a sentence of praise attributed to a real doctor who never said it is a fabrication about that person, not a piece of marketing copy.

Why did you choose preventive medicine?

The question every applicant asks a resident before they ask anyone else. Put to the four alumni above, and answered by them, not by us.

This is the question that decides applications, and it is the one most easily faked. Four answers drafted by a programme office would be four variations on the same sentence about impact at population scale. The four rows below stay empty until each alumnus writes their own, including any answer that begins with a reason an applicant might find unflattering.

External views

Two assessments from outside the programme. Neither is written yet, and neither will be written by us.

Everything above this line is either the programme describing itself or graduates describing the programme that trained them. Both have an interest in your answer. Internal testimony is not worthless, but it is the cheapest evidence on the page, and a reader who has weighed a few residency sites already knows to discount it.

What cannot be discounted is a judgement from someone who gains nothing if you apply and who carries a professional cost if the judgement is wrong: the ministry or statutory board that has hosted our residents and can compare them against every other placement it takes, and the external examiner who signs off the exit assessment. Those two statements would be worth more than every sentence above them, which is exactly why they are the hardest to obtain, and why the space is held open rather than filled with something easier to get.

A hosting ministry or board

Sought: a signed assessment from a named director at an organisation that has hosted our residents, on what a resident contributed and how that compared with other placements the organisation takes. Name, post, organisation and date all required before publication.

An external examiner

Sought: a statement from a named external examiner on the standard of the written and oral exit assessment. Name, institution, the years examined and date all required before publication.

Two statements sought, none yet received. Until they are, this section stays visibly empty rather than being filled with internal praise dressed as an outside view.
