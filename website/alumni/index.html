Four residents on what the training is like, and every graduate since 2011 by cohort and destination.

Thirty-eight doctors have completed this programme since 2011. Of those 38, the Programme Office can trace 36 to a current post. The two it cannot are counted in the denominator rather than dropped from it. Twenty-four residents are in training now.

Source: Programme Office records and the alumni tracing exercise, 2011–2026. Last verified 1 August 2026.

Quotations are the residents' own words, shortened but not rephrased. Posts, projects and prior training were confirmed with each of them before publication.

Why did you choose preventive medicine?

Four answers, in the residents' own words.

No two of them give the same reason.

Dr Nicholas Hang, 2025

“I chose Preventive Medicine because I want to impact health beyond individual patient encounters — focusing on populations and communities instead. For anyone interested in population health, this program provides solid training and exposes you to career paths you probably haven't considered. The field offers more options than most people realise.”

Dr Sean Lim, 2024

“During my surgical rotations, I was struck by the endless stream of patients with gangrenous limbs and amputations, often the result of years of poorly controlled diabetes. As I traced their journeys upstream, I saw the challenges primary care providers faced: managing complex chronic conditions within the limits of a short consultation. That is when I realized that healthcare makes up only 20% of a person's lifetime health. The other 80% unfolds long before a patient ever steps into a clinic.”

Dr Yin Zhien, 2022

“The preventive medicine residency offers me a unique opportunity to contribute meaningfully to policy and systemic change through hands-on involvement in diverse projects across rotations. Beyond clinical knowledge, the programme stretches my thinking through exposure to public health, health services planning, and systems-level decision-making.”

Dr Leonara Liu, 2018

“I chose to join the National Preventive Medicine Residency Programme as I wanted to work at the systems level, to effect change that would benefit the health of communities and populations. Since rotations are tailored to each individual resident's interests, this translates into a heterogeneity of experiences among residents.”

All four quotations are from the programme brochure, where they appear as Resident Testimonials. Each has been shortened; none has been rephrased.

External views

Two assessments from outside the programme.

Everything above this point is the programme describing itself, or residents describing the programme that trained them. The two statements below come from outside it: one from an organisation that has hosted our residents alongside placements from other training routes, the other from an examiner who has sat the exit assessment four times.

A hosting organisation

“We have taken residents from this programme into the division every year since 2019, alongside attachments from three other training routes. What distinguishes them is that they arrive able to read a surveillance dataset and leave having drafted something we could actually put to a policy committee. Two of the exposure-standard reviews we ran in the last three years began as resident work.”
Dr Priya Devadas, Director, Occupational Safety and Health Division, Ministry of Manpower. March 2026.

An external examiner

“I have examined the Part 2 portfolio viva for four sittings. The standard is comparable to the public health exit assessments I have examined in Australia and the United Kingdom, and the portfolio requirement is more demanding than either. Candidates are not passed on the strength of the presentation alone; the viva goes to the work behind it.”
Prof Alison Kerrigan, School of Population Health, University of Melbourne. External examiner, 2022–2025. February 2026.

Both statements were provided in writing and are published with their authors' consent. Neither author is employed by the programme or by the Sponsoring Institution.
